# The Problem

Education systems are evolving, but not quickly enough to match the pace of technological change. Across schools, there are consistent and widening gaps in digital skills, confidence, and opportunity.

### Uneven digital literacy

Students do not enter education with the same level of access or experience. Some have regular exposure to digital tools, coding, and structured learning outside the classroom. Others have minimal experience beyond basic device usage. This creates unequal starting points that are rarely addressed in a structured way.

### Limited applied learning

Much of digital education remains theoretical. Students are often taught concepts without consistent opportunities to apply them in practical, real-world contexts. As a result, confidence in using technology does not always match knowledge of it.

### Unclear career pathways

Many students have limited understanding of how digital careers actually work. Roles in software, cybersecurity, data, and technology are often poorly explained or inconsistently represented, making progression into these fields feel inaccessible or unclear.

### Lack of student-informed design

Educational programmes are frequently designed without direct input from students. This can lead to interventions that do not reflect real classroom experiences, barriers, or motivations.

These challenges combine to create a system where opportunity is unevenly distributed, particularly in digital and technology-focused subjects. Our programmes exist to explore and address these challenges through small, practical interventions.
